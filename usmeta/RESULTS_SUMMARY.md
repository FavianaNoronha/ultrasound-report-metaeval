# Results summary (plain language, not a paper)

Question: **do the scores used to grade AI-written ultrasound reports catch real mistakes, or do they only count matching words?** We planted one mistake at a time in reports (wrong BI-RADS, wrong side, wrong size, an invented or missed finding), made harmless rewordings of the same reports, and asked each scorer to rank "mistake" below "harmless". AUROC is the score for that: 1.0 = always ranks the mistake lower, 0.5 = coin flip, below 0.5 = prefers the mistake.

All final numbers are from the locked test split (tuning was on dev/val only). Every number below comes from a saved results file; the full table is `results_layers_all_final.md`, the step folders are listed in `results/partA/INDEX.md`, and the caveats are in `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`. Please read the limits section before quoting anything.

## Headline findings (updated 2026-11-13 to integrate Steps 2-38; the sections below keep the original Step 2-17 detail and the dated addenda unchanged)

1. **Scorers that count words do not track planted clinical errors.** They react to how much text changed, not to whether a fact changed. At the cut-off that flags 90% of errors, BERTScore also flags 84-92% of error-free candidates (Step 8b); against a matched one-word synonym the word scorers sit around chance (Step 10d); even at the same small edit size (0-5%) they still mostly prefer the error (breast strict preference BLEU 0.58, ROUGE-L 0.06, chrF 0.28, BERTScore 0.20, RaTEScore 0.33, Step 21e); against 29 human rewordings their AUROC is 0.08-0.17 (Step 21d, too small to compare); their score drop follows our provisional severity only weakly (Spearman 0.23-0.41 pooled, Step 30); and on errors a real writer made they do not separate wrong-fact reports from correct ones (AUROC 0.18-0.41, Step 28, inconclusive, see 6). **The pre-registered 2x2 factorial (Step 33) shows why:** their score is driven by wording, not by the error (variance share of wording vs fact: BLEU 0.60 vs 0.02, ROUGE-L 0.41 vs 0.02, chrF 0.27 vs 0.04, BERTScore 0.41 vs 0.02, RaTEScore 0.17 vs 0.04), an error with a small edit outranks a correct large rewrite in 76% of pairs (BLEU, ROUGE-L, chrF, BERTScore), and edit size alone explains BLEU and ROUGE-L (R² 0.62 and 0.71, Step 33 edit-size law). None of them reaches AUROC 0.80 for any size error up to x2 or any head-circumference shift up to 20 mm (Step 35); they break unit and synonym invariance (mean violation BLEU 60%, ROUGE-L 48%, chrF 36%, RaTEScore 35%), and BERTScore looks invariant (5%) only because it also fails to score an error lower than a synonym swap (Step 34). RadGraph-F1 behaves the same way (wording-driven, never reaches 0.80, Step 37).
2. **An AI judge (Gemma-2-9B) is the best text-only scorer here but is fragile.** AUROC 0.89-1.00 against a one-word synonym on all 10 datasets (59 cases each) and 0.60-0.71 against bigger harmless rewordings on breast and DDTI (Step 12); it is repeatable but not prompt-stable (swapping the order or rewording the instructions changes `judge_score` by more than 0.1 in 18-26% of pairs, Step 21c); its penalty score is floored at 0.00 for 171 of 179 long free-text reports (Step 28); against small rewordings it gets 0.91 / 0.89 / 0.94 at severity 1 / 2 / 3 (Step 30). In the factorial it is **fact-driven** (judge_score fact 0.68 vs wording 0.01; an error with a small edit outranks a correct large rewrite in 4% of pairs, Step 33), and it reaches AUROC 0.80 at the smallest error tested (5% size, 1 mm head circumference; small cells, Step 35). Simple wording edits do not fool it on average: the pre-registered adversarial hypothesis (a gain of at least 0.2) is not supported (mean gain -0.13, Step 36), although single reports can move by 0.3-0.5. It does not treat mm and cm alike (unit invariance fails, Step 34, small subset). One judge, one prompt, small samples; GREEN could not be run on this 16 GB machine (Step 37), so no GREEN-vs-judge statement exists.
3. **Fact extraction plus a fixed comparison catches the errors on templated text, and an LLM extractor does it without our template wording; on free text it is not reliable yet.** The regex `finding_f1` is 1.00 (an upper bound: it reads the template that wrote the reference). The Qwen2.5-7B extractor behind `llm_finding_f1` gets AUROC 0.99 / 1.00 / 1.00 against small / matched / large harmless rewordings, identical copies 1.0000, and changes only 0.2-1.6% of fields under reworded prompts (Step 27, templated text, 590 cases). On 30 hand-checked free-text reports it was right in 22 (the 12 fetal-head ones only because they were empty) and in only 10 of 18 breast and thyroid reports (56%, Step 28). A stricter prompt (extractor v3, Step 32) keeps templated agreement above 0.99 and removes invented fields (pathology, halo, count, composition) but also drops some stated ones; its free-text accuracy is **provisional until a real independent human hand check** of `docs/step32_check.csv`.
4. **The image check works only if the lesion is found, and a small label-free segmenter is enough on these datasets.** With the true box it gives position AUROC 0.97 and a correct-report score of 0.937 (Steps 16, 20, 24); with label-free boxes it collapses to chance (centre 0.54, whole image 0.47, random 0.47, wrong region 0.39, Step 21a; 0.31-0.54 under the tolerance rule, Step 24); it survives shrunk, enlarged and jittered boxes down to box IoU 0.09 as long as the centre stays on the lesion and falls when the centre moves (shift 50% of the box width 0.85, 75% 0.80, Step 24); size claims degrade earlier (below 0.80 at box IoU about 0.6, mostly mechanical). A U-Net trained on dev only, with no box, gives position AUROC 0.89 (0.84-0.93) pooled on the same 228 scans (breast 0.94, thyroid 0.80 with interval 0.69-0.89, on the 0.80 boundary), Dice 0.78 breast and 0.75 thyroid (Step 29). Across datasets (breast, leave-one-dataset-out, Step 31) the pooled position AUROC is 0.95 against 0.94 in-dataset; per source dataset it is lowest on TN5000 (0.69, absent from training) and DDTI (0.77), and scanner identity exists only in BUS-BRA (Step 38; per-dataset cells too small to compare). Abstaining when the U-Net mask is empty or low-confidence cuts mask failures from 12% to 4% of scans at 80% coverage; noise, blur, JPEG and gamma shifts lower the position AUROC by at most 0.05 (Step 38). Same-dataset test for Step 29, five datasets without patient ids, one seed. For fetal head, head circumference from U-Net masks has MAE 2.70 mm and the exact rule detects planted HC errors (0.87-0.92) but not 1 mm ones; the tolerance rule (tau 0.3, tuned on breast and thyroid size) cannot see HC errors at all (0.52, even with the labelled HC); oracle-geometry rows are circular; FetalHead has no pixel size and is not testable.
5. **Today's small open report writers get the facts wrong, and the scorers do not agree on which writer is better** (Qwen2.5-VL-3B and MedGemma-1.5-4B, Steps 11, 14; on 60 breast reports BI-RADS is right in 3 of 24 stated and size in 3 of 11, Step 28). On real clinician-written HiSBreast conclusions (101 of 427 machine-translated exams) the direction is the same (Step 15).
6. **Whether any scorer tracks real mistakes on free text is NOT established (Step 28, inconclusive).** On 180 test images (60 per organ, not the 100 planned) AUROC for separating reports with at least one wrong key fact from fully correct ones is 0.41 (BLEU), 0.40 (ROUGE-L), 0.33 (chrF), 0.18 (BERTScore), 0.41 (RaTEScore), 0.59 (`llm_finding_f1`), 0.58 (judge `sentence_f1`), none clearly above chance; but the labels come from an extractor that was 56% right on breast and thyroid reports, fetal head was not checkable (the writer states no head circumference or plane), and thyroid had 5 wrong reports. The decision, that scorers track similarity and completeness rather than mistakes, is indicative for breast only.
7. **Robustness checks did not overturn these findings.** Dropping test images whose near-duplicate cluster (<= 8 bits, loose) reaches dev or val changes no headline AUROC by more than 0.05 (largest +0.033, Step 21g); at the 4-bit threshold the splits were built with, no near-duplicate cluster and no patient / exam group spans splits (Step 21f); omission of a stated finding is a weak spot for the word scorers (AUROC 0.33-0.67 breast and thyroid, Step 25); an error-size dose-response shows BLEU and ROUGE-L flat in the size of an error (Step 21b).
8. **Severity levels are our own provisional labels and are confounded with error type and edit size** (Step 30): no per-level result can be read as "how clinically bad"; the image check is best at the critical level (0.99-1.00) mainly because that level is mostly lesion presence.

**In one line:** scores that count words (and RadGraph-F1) cannot be trusted to flag clinical errors, because their score follows wording rather than facts (Steps 33-35, 37); the Gemma judge, fact extraction (rules or an LLM) and an image check catch planted errors on templated text, but their validity on real free-text mistakes is not shown (Step 28, extractor v3 provisional), and the image check needs the lesion to be found, which a small segmenter does on these datasets, also across breast datasets and under mild corruptions (Steps 29, 31, 38). Every number comes from a saved results file listed in `results/partA/INDEX.md`; the caveats are in `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`.


### Steps 31-38 in short (numbers copied from `results/partA/step3x/results.md`)

**Status in one line:** Step 28 (natural errors) is inconclusive; Step 31 is breast only; Step 32 is provisional until a real independent human hand check; GREEN was not run; the hybrid evaluator was not run. We found no ultrasound work that tests the scorers with planted errors, severity and harmless rewordings; search not exhaustive.

- **Step 31, cross-dataset segmenter (breast only).** Leave-one-dataset-out on BrEaST, BUS-BRA, BUSI, UDIAT and BUS-UCLM (138 held-out test scans): pooled position AUROC 0.95 (0.91-0.98) against 0.94 (0.90-0.97) for the in-dataset Step 29 model on the same scans. Per held-out dataset (30 scans each, every cell too small to compare): BrEaST 0.94, BUS-BRA 0.97, BUSI 0.87, UDIAT 1.00, BUS-UCLM 0.94. Thyroid and fetal head were not run.
- **Step 32, stricter extractor v3 (provisional).** The prompt-only change keeps templated field agreement at 0.9975 (dev) and 0.9986 (val). Replaying the Step 28 notes, invented fields (pathology, halo, count, composition) disappear, but 7 of the 10 breast / thyroid extractions marked right for v2 change under v3. On the same 180 reports the pooled wrong-vs-correct AUROC moves by at most 0.05 (llm_finding_f1 0.60). Provisional until a real independent human hand check of `docs/step32_check.csv`; the AI-assisted review (16 right / 14 wrong) is a second rater only.
- **Step 33, edit-size law and 2x2 factorial (fact x wording, pre-registered).** Edit size explains BLEU (R² 0.62) and ROUGE-L (R² 0.71) but not chrF (0.21), BERTScore (0.45), RaTEScore (0.01) or judge_score (0.35). In the factorial, variance share of fact vs wording is BLEU 0.02 vs 0.60, ROUGE-L 0.02 vs 0.41, chrF 0.04 vs 0.27, BERTScore 0.02 vs 0.41, RaTEScore 0.04 vs 0.17 (wording-driven), and judge_score 0.68 vs 0.01, finding_f1 0.26 vs 0.00 (fact-driven). An error with a small edit outranks a correct large rewrite in 76% of pairs for BLEU, ROUGE-L, chrF and BERTScore, 60% for RaTEScore, 4% for judge_score (judge subset: 71 groups).
- **Step 34, metamorphic checks.** Mean violation of the unit / order / synonym invariances (change > 0.05): BLEU 60%, ROUGE-L 48%, chrF 36%, RaTEScore 35%, judge_score 30% (subset), finding_f1 6%, BERTScore 5%, llm_finding_f1 5%. Failure to score a number swap or negation flip below a harmless synonym swap: ROUGE-L 93%, chrF 79%, BERTScore 64%, RaTEScore 60%, finding_f1 44%, BLEU 40%, llm_finding_f1 39%, judge_score 29%. The 41 HiSBreast texts (local only) give mostly too-small cells. The earlier hybrid-evaluator pre-registration (`step34_hybrid`) was not run.
- **Step 35, limit of detection.** Smallest error with AUROC >= 0.80 against matched synonyms: BLEU, ROUGE-L, chrF, BERTScore and RaTEScore never reach it (size errors up to x2, head-circumference shifts up to 20 mm); judge_score reaches it at 5% (size) and 1 mm (head circumference), on small judge cells; judge sentence_f1 25% (thyroid) to 100% (breast, % size); oracle-box MedSAM image check 25%; label-free U-Net check 50%; regex finding_f1 (upper bound) 5% and 1 mm.
- **Step 36, adversarial wording attack on the judge (pre-registered).** Hypothesis (a wording-only gain of at least 0.2) not supported: the winning transform (reorder) on 100 test wrong reports changed the judge score by -0.13 (-0.18 to -0.09) on average, 5% gained >= 0.2, and 6% overtook their correct counterpart (9% before). On dev / val all five transforms lowered the mean score (-0.10 to -0.21); single reports moved by up to +0.3 to +0.5.
- **Step 37, RadGraph-F1 added; GREEN not run.** RadGraph-F1 is wording-driven (fact 0.00 vs wording 0.17 on 17,444 blocks), never reaches AUROC 0.80, violates unit invariance on 100% and synonym invariance on 90% of templated items, and does not beat the judge. GREEN was not run: the official 13 GB checkpoint did not finish scoring two pairs in 36 minutes on the 16 GB machine; no GREEN number exists and nothing was substituted.
- **Step 38, responsible-AI checks on the image check.** Position AUROC per source dataset (30 scans each, too small to compare): TN5000 0.69, DDTI 0.77, BUSI 0.84, BrEaST 0.91, TN3K 0.93, BUS-UCLM 0.94, BUS-BRA 1.00, UDIAT 1.00; scanner identity exists in the files of BUS-BRA only (4 device codes). Abstaining on low U-Net confidence lowers the position-check error from 4.8% to 3.3% at 80% coverage and 2.2% at 60%, and mask failure (Dice < 0.5) from 12.1% to 3.8% and 1.7%. Noise, blur, JPEG and gamma shifts lower the position AUROC by at most 0.05 (clean 0.89, worst 0.84).

## Findings from Steps 2-17 (original detail; superseded in part by the headline above)

1. **Word-overlap scorers and BERTScore cannot be set to a threshold that catches mistakes without flagging harmless rewordings.** At the cut-off that flags 90% of planted errors (chosen on val), BERTScore also flags 84–92% of error-free candidates and keeps only 0–6% of harmless ones; BLEU keeps 0–50% (Step 8b, test, 10 datasets). A one-line lazy answer, "No abnormality.", scores 0.84–0.86 on BERTScore for the templated BrEaST, BUS-BRA and BUSI reports and 0.86 on real HiSBreast text (Steps 10d, 15).
2. **What word-overlap scorers report depends on how much text a reword changes, not on whether a fact changed.** Planted errors alter 2–4% of the words of a templated report; harmless rewordings alter 5–60%. Against a harmless reword that is as small as an error (one-word synonym), BLEU and ROUGE-L rise above chance (BrEaST 0.61 and 0.58, BUS-BRA 0.75 and 0.66) while chrF stays below it (0.25, 0.36) and BERTScore sits at about chance (0.53, 0.42) (Step 10d, test). So the useful comparison is the matched-synonym column, not the "small" or "large" ones.
3. **An AI judge (Gemma-2-9B) reliably separates a mistake from a one-word reword (AUROC 0.89–1.00 on all 10 datasets, 59 cases each), but is weak against bigger harmless rewordings on breast and DDTI** (0.60–0.71 against the "small" group: BUS-BRA 0.60, UDIAT 0.61, BrEaST 0.67, BUSI 0.67, DDTI 0.71; Step 12). It is repeatable with greedy decoding (0 of 60 pairs changed over 5 runs, Step 8b) but **depends on the prompt** (up to 0.30 AUROC between two prompts on 20 cases per dataset, Step 7) and on the judge's model family (below).
4. **A structured fact check (`finding_f1`) catches every planted error on templated text (1.00) but that is an upper bound**, because the extractor reads the same template that wrote the reference. On free text written by AI models it mostly measures "not stated": 0.18–0.30 on BrEaST, BUS-BRA, TN3K and TN5000 and 0.00 on HC18 (Steps 11, 14; 14–22 reports per dataset).
5. **An image check needs predicted masks to mean anything.** With the true masks it scores 1.00 on position and size errors by construction (the reference came from the same masks). With masks predicted by MedSAM it still separates planted errors from harmless rewordings on breast and thyroid (AUROC 0.91 pooled over 228 cases, 0.82–0.98 per dataset; the text scorers on the same cases score 0.25–0.62). **Read this as the easy case, for three reasons.** (a) The box prompts came from the TRUE masks (oracle localisation): the segmenter was told where the lesion is. The realistic case, a model finding the lesion itself, is not tested, because the Qwen and MedGemma boxes were too poor to use as prompts (Steps 11, 14). (b) The planted size errors are large (×0.5 to ×2), so they are easy to see even with a rough mask. (c) 30 scans per dataset, test split only. On **fetal head** the check is no better than the text scorers (AUROC 0.46 and 0.50). The mismatch is between the true skull outline (the labelled mask includes the bright skull ring) and MedSAM's inner-brain outline, so the head circumference comes out too small: a ground-truth/segmenter disagreement, **not proof that image checks fail on fetal head** (a segmenter trained on the same outline convention was not tried). The check also **marks correct reports down**: mean score of a correct report is 0.68 over all 10 datasets (0.71–0.94 on breast/thyroid, about 0 on the fetal-head sets) (Step 16).
6. **Today's small open report writers are poor at the facts, and the scorers do not agree on which writer is better.** Qwen2.5-VL-3B and MedGemma-1.5-4B (4-bit) get BI-RADS, side and pathology right only occasionally (e.g. Qwen BrEaST BI-RADS exact match 0 of 19), their boxes overlap the lesion poorly (IoU 0.12–0.30 except on HC18, 0.64–0.77), word-overlap scores differ between them by 0.00–0.02 (BLEU, BERTScore), and the Qwen judge scores MedGemma far lower than the Gemma judge scores Qwen (sentence_f1 gaps 0.12–0.36), so no scorer gives a trustworthy ranking (Steps 11, 14; every dataset n < 30, so these rows are descriptive).
7. **On real clinician-written conclusions (HiSBreast, a small machine-translated subset) the direction is the same:** BERTScore is at chance (0.43, 41 test exams) and gives the lazy answer 0.86; BLEU, ROUGE-L and chrF look acceptable against structural edits (0.64–0.76, helped by the same edit-size effect as in finding 2) and collapse against an LLM paraphrase of the same text (0.04–0.24, n=21); the Qwen2.5-7B judge reaches 0.79–0.89 against all harmless groups, including the paraphrase (0.81 and 0.89, n=21; Step 15).

## Findings that do NOT hold, or are artefacts

- **The Step 4 headline "word-overlap scorers are below chance" is retracted.** It was mainly the edit-size effect: our harmless rewordings changed 5–10 times more text than a planted error (Steps 5, 5 rerun2; `CHANGELOG.md`).
- **"Layer 1 and Layer 3 score 1.00" is not evidence that they work.** On templated text they read the same template and masks that built the reference (upper bound, templated). The only non-circular image result is Step 16.
- **The first judge number (BrEaST 0.86 against small rewordings) was optimistic.** On the locked test split it is 0.60 with 25 cases and 0.67 (0.59–0.75) with 59.
- **Judge error-type counts say more about the judge than about the writer:** the Gemma judge names "false finding" most often for Qwen reports, the Qwen judge names "missed finding" most often for MedGemma reports (Step 14).
- **Ranking the two writers with `finding_f1` or `sentence_f1` is not valid**: different model families graded them (a judge never grades its own family), so the two columns are different rulers.
- **BrEaST being the "cleanest" check for training exposure is a guess**, not a finding: Qwen does no better there than on older sets, which neither supports nor rules out exposure (Step 11 rerun2).
- **Fixed bugs that changed earlier numbers** (all kept in the changelog, reruns kept): BUS-BRA "single breast" wording (Step 5 rerun3), extractor decimal bug and copied option lists (Step 11b), `box_support` identical-copy failure (replaced by `box_support_gtbox`).
- **In Step 16 the jittered prompts scored slightly higher than the exact box** (IoU 0.65 and 0.64 vs 0.62). We did not test why; it is not a finding that worse prompts help.

## Still unverified

- **Whether any scorer tracks real mistakes in free text** (Step 28 is inconclusive: noisy extractor labels, fetal head not checkable, 60 images per organ), and the extractor's free-text accuracy beyond the 30-report hand check (22 of 30 right, 10 of 18 on breast and thyroid).
- **Whether the label-free U-Net generalises** beyond the datasets it was trained on (only TN5000, 30 scans, is cross-dataset) and beyond one seed (Step 29).
- Whether any of this holds for **real clinician-written ultrasound reports at scale**: only HiSBreast has them (101 of 427 exams usable), and none of the 10 image datasets does.
- **Clinician confirmation** of the severity ladder, and of the template sources (marked "to confirm").
- **Whether the report writers or MedSAM saw these public datasets** (unverifiable; the memorisation probes found nothing, which proves nothing).
- **Bilingual review of the HiSBreast translations** (nobody on the team reads Vietnamese; the checks are automatic).
- Not run: GREEN, a Claude judge, the original RadFact, Jev (paid API, not approved), LLaVA-Med, a detector that finds the lesion instead of being given the box.
- The extractor accuracy on free text is a regex proxy on 10–40 reports per set, not a labelled test.

## Limits

- **Templated references.** Every reference report in the 10 image datasets is built by our templates from labels and masks: synthetic but fact-correct. Planted errors are single, large and clean (e.g. size ×0.5 to ×2), larger than most real mistakes.
- **Provisional severity.** Severity levels are our own guesses pending a clinician; severity correlations and critical-only AUROC depend on them.
- **No clinician** has reviewed any report, error or ranking.
- **HiSBreast is a machine-translated subset:** 101 of 427 breast exams (24%) passed three automatic translation checks, the keyword dictionary was written by the assistant, and the kept subset is easier than average. Text and translations stay local (licence blank).
- **Possible training exposure** of the report writers (Qwen2.5-VL, MedGemma) and of MedSAM to BUSI, BUS-BRA, TN3K, TN5000, HC18 and others, unverifiable; pretrained scorers (BERTScore, RaTEScore, the judges) cannot be decontaminated by us.
- **No patient ids for 5 datasets** (BUSI, UDIAT, TN3K, TN5000, HC18): groups are images, so the same patient may sit in two splits. BUS-UCLM's test split has only 9 patients. Near-duplicates inside a dataset are grouped, not dropped.
- **Small samples.** Judge 59 cases per dataset; VLM reports 14–22 per dataset; Part B 30 scans per dataset (18 for BUS-UCLM); HiSBreast 41 test exams. Rows with fewer than 30 cases or groups are marked "too small to compare". Results are per dataset; pooled rows only where stated.
- **One judge model per family, one prompt per run, greedy decoding, one machine** (16 GB Mac, 4-bit local models). Dataset licences are "to confirm" and images are not in the repository.

## Related work

We found no ultrasound work that tests the scorers with planted errors, severity and harmless rewordings (our search was not exhaustive). Nothing here is a claim of being first. The ideas we extend were developed elsewhere, mostly on chest X-ray, and are not ours: ReEvalMed, "Beyond Scalar Scores" (arXiv 2606.18797; discrimination versus robustness of report metrics; chest X-ray), the ReXErr cross-dataset study (Naidu et al., medRxiv 2026), "Can Jev Judge Radiology Reports?" (arXiv 2609.27607; judge repeatability and calibration), the breast-ultrasound report-auditing study (J Digit Imaging 2026, doi 10.1007/s10278-026-02163-z; false-alarm rate, report- versus error-level sensitivity), VLScore (arXiv 2410.17357), and the metrics we ran or borrowed from: BLEU, ROUGE-L, chrF, BERTScore, RaTEScore, GREEN (not run), CRIMSON (2603.06183), RadMatch (2609.01470), RadEval, RadFact-style sentence checks, UltraG-Bench (2609.30928; SemAcc), U2-Bench (2505.17779) and BUSTR (2511.20956). **Citation status:** only VLScore's title, authors and venue were checked against its arXiv page; every other reference is as named in the project brief and has not been re-read here (the ReXErr citation details in particular are unconfirmed). Verify each before it goes in a paper (`results/partA/step8b_borrowed/related_work.md`).

- **19a, paired preference, ties and cut-offs (saved test scores; `results/partA/step19a_pairs/`).** Word-overlap scorers strictly prefer the harmless rewording over the error in only 0.38–0.61 of pairs on breast, 0.26–0.45 on thyroid and 0.00–0.29 on fetal head; ROUGE-L ties in 20–43% of pairs. At a val cut-off that flags 90% of planted errors, they also flag 0.69–1.00 of harmless rewordings on test. The judge prefers the rewording in 0.92–0.97 (pref_half) with 0.01–0.08 false alarms, but a single pooled cut-off catches only 34% of fetal-head errors. `finding_f1` (1.00 / 0.00) is an upper bound on templated text. Cut-offs do not transfer reliably between datasets of the same organ (single-pair changes in false-alarm rate from −0.72 to +0.62). Breast, thyroid and fetal head are never pooled together.
- **19b, box-degradation sweep for the image check (`results/partA/step19b_boxsweep/`).** A degraded-prompt sweep, **not a detector**: all boxes still come from the true mask. The exact box reproduces Step 16 (AUROC 0.91). Position errors stay at 0.90–0.96 under every degradation; size errors fall from 0.86 to 0.76 (box shrunk 10%), 0.53 (25%) and 0.40 (50%, below chance), so the image check's advantage over text on size errors depends on a good box. A 25% larger box is better than the exact box (0.96). Fetal head stays near 0.5 in every condition.
- **19c, dataset manifest table (`datasets/MANIFEST_TABLE.md`).** Licences, URLs and citations are all "to confirm"; five datasets have no patient/exam id; near-duplicates range from 0% to 37% (BUSI); candidates built from the same original image are not independent (13–31 rows per image), so all intervals resample groups.

## Addendum: Steps 18–20 (2026-11-03)

- **Step 18 — exploratory, synthetic size, DDTI only (`results/partA/step18_threshold_crossing/`).** No dataset has both a real size in mm and a TI-RADS level, so sizes are synthetic (real TI-RADS level). For equal-size errors where one crosses a TI-RADS cut-off and the other does not, BLEU, ROUGE-L and `finding_f1` give identical scores, chrF and BERTScore differ by ≤ 0.001, `sentence_f1` shows no difference; RaTEScore (+0.015) and `judge_score` (+0.024, lower CI bound +0.0004) tilt slightly toward penalising the crossing error. Pre-registered verdicts: H1 falsified (tiny effect, 4 of 5 scorers held), H2 partly held, H3 (BrEaST, 20 mm) not supported (MedSAM median error 2.0 mm; 1 of 39 cases lands on the other side of 20 mm), H4 not run. Reading: scorers mostly register "a size differs", not "a size that matters". Not a finding about real nodule sizes.
- **Step 19 (routing table, saved test results).** 22 of 43 dataset × category cells have ≥ 30 cases; the judge has the highest score in 20 of them (clearly above word scorers in 19); `finding_f1` is an upper bound; image check beats text on size/position for breast + thyroid but not fetal head.

## Addendum: Step 21 robustness tests (2026-11-04; new sections only)

## Addendum: Step 36, adversarial wording attack on the judge (2026-11-12; new section only; pre-registered)
