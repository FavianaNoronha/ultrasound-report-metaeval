# Results summary (plain language, not a paper)

Question: **do the scores used to grade AI-written ultrasound reports catch real mistakes, or do they only count matching words?** We planted one mistake at a time in reports (wrong BI-RADS, wrong side, wrong size, an invented or missed finding), made harmless rewordings of the same reports, and asked each scorer to rank "mistake" below "harmless". AUROC is the score for that: 1.0 = always ranks the mistake lower, 0.5 = coin flip, below 0.5 = prefers the mistake.

All final numbers are from the locked test split (tuning was on dev/val only). Every number below comes from a saved results file; the full table is `results_layers_all_final.md`, the step folders are listed in `results/partA/INDEX.md`, and the caveats are in `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`. Please read the limits section before quoting anything.

## Findings that hold

1. **Word-overlap scorers and BERTScore cannot be set to a threshold that catches mistakes without flagging harmless rewordings.** At the cut-off that flags 90% of planted errors (chosen on val), BERTScore also flags 84–92% of error-free candidates and keeps only 0–6% of harmless ones; BLEU keeps 0–50% (Step 8b, test, 10 datasets). A one-line lazy answer, "No abnormality.", scores 0.84–0.86 on BERTScore for the templated BrEaST, BUS-BRA and BUSI reports and 0.86 on real HiSBreast text (Steps 10d, 15).
2. **What word-overlap scorers report depends on how much text a reword changes, not on whether a fact changed.** Planted errors alter 2–4% of the words of a templated report; harmless rewordings alter 5–60%. Against a harmless reword that is as small as an error (one-word synonym), BLEU and ROUGE-L rise above chance (BrEaST 0.61 and 0.58, BUS-BRA 0.75 and 0.66) while chrF stays below it (0.25, 0.36) and BERTScore sits at about chance (0.53, 0.42) (Step 10d, test). So the useful comparison is the matched-synonym column, not the "small" or "large" ones.
3. **An AI judge (Gemma-2-9B) reliably separates a mistake from a one-word reword (AUROC 0.89–1.00 on all 10 datasets, 59 cases each), but is weak against bigger harmless rewordings on breast and DDTI** (0.60–0.71 against the "small" group: BUS-BRA 0.60, UDIAT 0.61, BrEaST 0.67, BUSI 0.67, DDTI 0.71; Step 12). It is repeatable with greedy decoding (0 of 60 pairs changed over 5 runs, Step 8b) but **depends on the prompt** (up to 0.30 AUROC between two prompts on 20 cases per dataset, Step 7) and on the judge's model family (below).
4. **A structured fact check (`finding_f1`) catches every planted error on templated text (1.00) but that is an upper bound**, because the extractor reads the same template that wrote the reference. On free text written by AI models it mostly measures "not stated": 0.18–0.30 on BrEaST, BUS-BRA, TN3K and TN5000 and 0.00 on HC18 (Steps 11, 14; 14–22 reports per dataset).
5. **An image check needs predicted masks to mean anything.** With the true masks it scores 1.00 on position and size errors by construction (the reference came from the same masks). With masks predicted by MedSAM it still separates planted errors from harmless rewordings on breast and thyroid (AUROC 0.91 pooled over 228 cases, 0.82–0.98 per dataset; the text scorers on the same cases score 0.25–0.62). **Read this as the easy case, for three reasons.** (a) The box prompts came from the TRUE masks (oracle localisation): the segmenter was told where the lesion is. The realistic case, a model finding the lesion itself, is not tested, because the Qwen and MedGemma boxes were too poor to use as prompts (Steps 11, 14). (b) The planted size errors are large (×0.5 to ×2), so they are easy to see even with a rough mask. (c) 30 scans per dataset, test split only. On **fetal head** the check is no better than the text scorers (AUROC 0.46 and 0.50). The mismatch is between the true skull outline (the labelled mask includes the bright skull ring) and MedSAM's inner-brain outline, so the head circumference comes out too small: a ground-truth/segmenter disagreement, **not proof that image checks fail on fetal head** (a segmenter trained on the same outline convention was not tried). The check also **marks correct reports down**: mean score of a correct report is 0.68 over all 10 datasets (0.71–0.94 on breast/thyroid, about 0 on the fetal-head sets) (Step 16).
6. **Today's small open report writers are poor at the facts, and the scorers do not agree on which writer is better.** Qwen2.5-VL-3B and MedGemma-1.5-4B (4-bit) get BI-RADS, side and pathology right only occasionally (e.g. Qwen BrEaST BI-RADS exact match 0 of 19), their boxes overlap the lesion poorly (IoU 0.12–0.30 except on HC18, 0.64–0.77), word-overlap scores differ between them by 0.00–0.02 (BLEU, BERTScore), and the Qwen judge scores MedGemma far lower than the Gemma judge scores Qwen (sentence_f1 gaps 0.12–0.36), so no scorer gives a trustworthy ranking (Steps 11, 14; every dataset n < 30, so these rows are descriptive).
7. **On real clinician-written conclusions (HiSBreast, a small machine-translated subset) the direction is the same:** BERTScore is at chance (0.43, 41 test exams) and gives the lazy answer 0.86; BLEU, ROUGE-L and chrF look acceptable against structural edits (0.64–0.76, helped by the same edit-size effect as in finding 2) and collapse against an LLM paraphrase of the same text (0.04–0.24, n=21); the Qwen2.5-7B judge reaches 0.79–0.89 against all harmless groups, including the paraphrase (0.81 and 0.89, n=21; Step 15).

## Findings that do NOT hold, or are artefacts

- **The Step 4 headline "word-overlap scorers are below chance" is retracted.** It was mainly the edit-size effect: our harmless rewordings changed 5–10 times more text than a planted error (Steps 5, 5 rerun2; `CHANGELOG.md`).
- **"Layer 1 and Layer 3 score 1.00" is not evidence that they work.** On templated text they read the same template and masks that built the reference (upper bound, templated). The only non-circular image result is Step 16.
- **The first judge number (BrEaST 0.86 against small rewordings) was optimistic.** On the locked test split it is 0.60 with 25 cases and 0.67 (0.59–0.75) with 59.
- **Judge error-type counts say more about the judge than about the writer:** the Gemma judge names "false finding" most often for Qwen reports, the Qwen judge names "missed finding" most often for MedGemma reports (Step 14).
- **Ranking the two writers with `finding_f1` or `sentence_f1` is not valid**: different model families graded them (a judge never grades its own family), so the two columns are different rulers.
- **BrEaST being the "cleanest" check for training exposure is a guess**, not a finding: Qwen does no better there than on older sets, which neither supports nor rules out exposure (Step 11 rerun2).
- **Fixed bugs that changed earlier numbers** (all kept in the changelog, reruns kept): BUS-BRA "single breast" wording (Step 5 rerun3), extractor decimal bug and copied option lists (Step 11b), `box_support` identical-copy failure (replaced by `box_support_gtbox`).
- **In Step 16 the jittered prompts scored slightly higher than the exact box** (IoU 0.65 and 0.64 vs 0.62). We did not test why; it is not a finding that worse prompts help.

## Still unverified

- Whether any of this holds for **real clinician-written ultrasound reports at scale**: only HiSBreast has them (101 of 427 exams usable), and none of the 10 image datasets does.
- **Clinician confirmation** of the severity ladder, and of the template sources (marked "to confirm").
- **Whether the report writers or MedSAM saw these public datasets** (unverifiable; the memorisation probes found nothing, which proves nothing).
- **Bilingual review of the HiSBreast translations** (nobody on the team reads Vietnamese; the checks are automatic).
- Not run: GREEN, a Claude judge, the original RadFact, Jev (paid API, not approved), LLaVA-Med, a detector that finds the lesion instead of being given the box.
- The extractor accuracy on free text is a regex proxy on 10–40 reports per set, not a labelled test.

## Limits

- **Templated references.** Every reference report in the 10 image datasets is built by our templates from labels and masks: synthetic but fact-correct. Planted errors are single, large and clean (e.g. size ×0.5 to ×2), larger than most real mistakes.
- **Provisional severity.** Severity levels are our own guesses pending a clinician; severity correlations and critical-only AUROC depend on them.
- **No clinician** has reviewed any report, error or ranking.
- **HiSBreast is a machine-translated subset:** 101 of 427 breast exams (24%) passed three automatic translation checks, the keyword dictionary was written by the assistant, and the kept subset is easier than average. Text and translations stay local (licence blank).
- **Possible training exposure** of the report writers (Qwen2.5-VL, MedGemma) and of MedSAM to BUSI, BUS-BRA, TN3K, TN5000, HC18 and others, unverifiable; pretrained scorers (BERTScore, RaTEScore, the judges) cannot be decontaminated by us.
- **No patient ids for 5 datasets** (BUSI, UDIAT, TN3K, TN5000, HC18): groups are images, so the same patient may sit in two splits. BUS-UCLM's test split has only 9 patients. Near-duplicates inside a dataset are grouped, not dropped.
- **Small samples.** Judge 59 cases per dataset; VLM reports 14–22 per dataset; Part B 30 scans per dataset (18 for BUS-UCLM); HiSBreast 41 test exams. Rows with fewer than 30 cases or groups are marked "too small to compare". Results are per dataset; pooled rows only where stated.
- **One judge model per family, one prompt per run, greedy decoding, one machine** (16 GB Mac, 4-bit local models). Dataset licences are "to confirm" and images are not in the repository.

## Related work

We found no ultrasound work that tests the scorers with planted errors, severity and harmless rewordings (our search was not exhaustive). Nothing here is a claim of being first. The ideas we extend were developed elsewhere, mostly on chest X-ray, and are not ours: ReEvalMed, "Beyond Scalar Scores" (arXiv 2606.18797; discrimination versus robustness of report metrics; chest X-ray), the ReXErr cross-dataset study (Naidu et al., medRxiv 2026), "Can Jev Judge Radiology Reports?" (arXiv 2609.27607; judge repeatability and calibration), the breast-ultrasound report-auditing study (J Digit Imaging 2026, doi 10.1007/s10278-026-02163-z; false-alarm rate, report- versus error-level sensitivity), VLScore (arXiv 2410.17357), and the metrics we ran or borrowed from: BLEU, ROUGE-L, chrF, BERTScore, RaTEScore, GREEN (not run), CRIMSON (2603.06183), RadMatch (2609.01470), RadEval, RadFact-style sentence checks, UltraG-Bench (2609.30928; SemAcc), U2-Bench (2505.17779) and BUSTR (2511.20956). **Citation status:** only VLScore's title, authors and venue were checked against its arXiv page; every other reference is as named in the project brief and has not been re-read here (the ReXErr citation details in particular are unconfirmed). Verify each before it goes in a paper (`results/partA/step8b_borrowed/related_work.md`).
