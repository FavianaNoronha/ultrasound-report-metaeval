## What was run, and the deviations you need to know about
- **Model:** `google/medgemma-1.5-4b-it` official weights (8.64 GB download approved, HF cache only). **bfloat16 on MPS did not fit comfortably in 16 GB** (the Mac swapped about 17 GB, 30-140 s per report), so, as you allowed, the results use a **4-bit MLX conversion made locally from the official weights** (`mlx_vlm convert -q --q-bits 4 --q-group-size 64`, 5.5 bits per weight on average, 3.2 GB, stored in `~/.cache/usmeta`, not in git). It needed Python 3.12 (installed with `uv` into `~/venvs/mlx312`; the project's Python 3.9 cannot run mlx-vlm for Gemma 3). **The 4-bit model is not bit-identical to bf16 MedGemma**; 6 reports from the abandoned bf16 attempts are kept (`reports_medgemma_bf16_partial*.jsonl`) but not used.
- **Same scans and same prompt as Qwen:** the 92 test scans of the Qwen run (BUS-BRA 21, BrEaST 19, HC18 14, TN3K 22, TN5000 16) plus the 10 flipped test copies, and 20 dev/val scans for checks. Prompt text and greedy decoding as in `usmeta/generate.py` (max 220 new tokens). Boxes asked with the same sentence as for Qwen ("Locate the ... bounding box in JSON format"; the 10 flipped copies were not boxed, time).
- **Judge and extractor are Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (4-bit MLX), never Gemma, for MedGemma's reports.** Qwen's reports were graded by Gemma-2-9B (Step 11). Consequence: **`finding_f1` and `sentence_check` are different measuring sticks for the two writers**, so differences in those columns mix writer quality and judge/extractor behaviour. Layer 0 scorers (BLEU, ROUGE-L, chrF, BERTScore, RaTEScore) and box IoU are family-free and are the fair basis for the ranking comparison.
- **Three adaptations of the frozen tools for the non-Gemma grader (all in CHANGELOG, chosen on dev/val only, Gemma/Qwen results unchanged):** (1) Qwen2.5-7B ignored the "{value, quote}" extractor format for 57 of 122 reports -> a format reminder was appended to the prompt (extractor v2.1; unparsed 10 of 122 afterwards). (2) It then shortened its quotes, so the verbatim-quote rule dropped many values: extractor dev/val proxy **30/39 = 76.9% (below your 85% stop rule; I stopped and you approved a looser rule)** -> quotes accepted when at least 80% of their words occur in the report (extractor v2.2): **35/39 = 89.7%** on the 20 MedGemma dev/val reports, tuned on those same reports, still only a regex proxy. (3) It copied the candidate's list length into both true/false lists (85/102 and 100/102 mismatched) -> `v1q` / `v2q` prompts = frozen prompt_v1 / prompt_v2 plus one line giving the exact sentence counts (mismatch 1 and 2 of 102; v1q left 5 replies unparseable, counted as missing).

**Error types named by the Qwen2.5-7B judge (prompt v2q, test counts):** missed finding is its most common label (BUS-BRA 42, BrEaST 60, TN3K 116, TN5000 91, HC18 53), then wrong location (BUS-BRA 25, BrEaST 17) and wrong measurement (BUS-BRA 20, BrEaST 14); false finding is rarer (5-23) -- the opposite pattern to the Gemma judge on Qwen's reports (where false finding dominated: 43-94). **Reading.** (1) **No, the scorers do not agree on a ranking.** The word-overlap and embedding scorers point (weakly) to MedGemma on most datasets but the gaps are tiny (BLEU 0.00-0.01, BERTScore 0.00-0.02, ROUGE-L and chrF 0.01-0.05); RaTEScore favours Qwen on the breast sets; the Qwen2.5-7B judge scores MedGemma far lower than the Gemma judge scores Qwen (sentence_f1 v1 gaps 0.22-0.36, v2 0.12-0.38 on thyroid and HC18); box IoU is a tie except HC18 (Qwen 0.77 vs MedGemma 0.64). (2) The judge gap is **not interpretable as writer quality**: the two judges are different models (Gemma-2-9B vs Qwen2.5-7B) with different strictness, so a judge-free scorer is needed to compare writers, and the judge-free scorers see almost no difference. A neutral third-family judge for both writers was not run (time). (3) The pooled and per-dataset rows were not tuned; all intervals come from 14-40 cases.
